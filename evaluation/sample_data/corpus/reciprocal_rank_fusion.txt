Reciprocal rank fusion, or RRF, is a way to combine the results of several
retrieval methods into a single ranked list.

The key insight is that the scores produced by different retrievers are not
directly comparable. A dense model returns cosine similarities between zero and
one, while a sparse model returns BM25 scores that can range widely and have no
natural bound. Instead of blending those raw scores, RRF converts each result
into a reciprocal rank and sums them.

For each document that appears in any of the result lists, RRF assigns a score
of 1 divided by a constant plus the document's position in that list. Documents
that rank highly in several lists accumulate a larger fused score, while
documents that appear in only one list rank lower. The constant smooths the
curve so that moving from rank one to rank two does not produce a huge jump.

Because RRF is purely rank based, no score calibration is required, which makes
it simple and robust in practice. This is why hybrid retrieval systems, which
combine dense and sparse results, almost always use reciprocal rank fusion
rather than trying to align the underlying scores.
