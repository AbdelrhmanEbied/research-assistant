Hybrid retrieval combines dense and sparse search in a single query pipeline.

Dense retrieval embeds the query and documents into vectors and finds
neighbours by cosine similarity. It understands meaning and synonyms but can
miss exact terms that were never seen during training. Sparse retrieval, often
BM25, matches on exact keyword overlap and captures rare or technical terms
reliably.

Hybrid search runs both approaches in parallel and fuses their ranked lists.
The most common fusion strategy is reciprocal rank fusion (RRF), which sums
1 / (k + rank) across the two result sets. Because RRF is rank-based rather
than score-based, the two methods can be combined without calibrating their
scores.

The result is a single ranked list where lexical precision and semantic
recall both contribute, which tends to outperform either method alone across
a wide range of queries.
